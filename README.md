# Walmart Sales Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes historical weekly sales data from 45 Walmart stores to identify store performance, sales trends, seasonality, holiday effects, sales variability, and relationships between weekly sales and external economic factors.

The project uses Python, pandas, and Matplotlib for data analysis and visualization, with processed outputs prepared for Power BI reporting.

---

## Business Objective

The objective of this project is to transform raw Walmart sales data into useful business insights that can support decisions related to:

- store performance
- seasonal planning
- holiday preparation
- inventory planning
- staffing decisions
- sales forecasting
- operational benchmarking

---

## Dataset

The dataset contains **6,435 store-week observations** across **45 Walmart stores**.

### Variables

- `Store` — Store identifier
- `Date` — Week associated with the sales record
- `Weekly_Sales` — Weekly sales generated by the store
- `Holiday_Flag` — Indicates whether the week is classified as a holiday week
- `Temperature` — Recorded temperature
- `Fuel_Price` — Fuel price during the week
- `CPI` — Consumer Price Index
- `Unemployment` — Unemployment rate

## Key Findings

### Store Performance

- Store 20 generated the highest total sales at approximately **$301.40 million**.
- Store 4 ranked second with approximately **$299.54 million**.
- Stores 14 and 13 also generated more than **$285 million** in total sales.
- All stores contained the same number of weekly observations, so total-sales and average-weekly-sales rankings were consistent.

### Holiday Sales

- Holiday weeks generated average store-week sales of approximately **$1.12 million**.
- Non-holiday weeks generated average store-week sales of approximately **$1.04 million**.
- Holiday weeks were associated with approximately **7.8% higher average weekly sales**.

### Seasonality

- Sales showed a clear recurring year-end pattern.
- November and December were among the strongest sales periods.
- December 2010 and December 2011 recorded average company-wide weekly sales of approximately **$58 million**.
- Sales declined sharply following the December peaks.

### Sales Variability

- Store 35 had the highest coefficient of variation at approximately **23.0%**.
- Stores 7 and 15 followed with relative sales variability of approximately **19.7%** and **19.3%**.
- Higher variability indicates less consistent weekly sales relative to a store's average sales level.

### Yearly Sales

Average company-wide weekly sales were approximately:

- **2010:** $47.69 million
- **2011:** $47.08 million
- **2012:** $46.51 million

The differences were relatively small. Because 2010 and 2012 contain fewer observed weeks than 2011, these values should not be interpreted as complete calendar-year growth comparisons.

### External Factors

Pearson correlations with `Weekly_Sales` were approximately:

| External Factor | Correlation |
|---|---:|
| Fuel Price | 0.009 |
| Temperature | -0.064 |
| CPI | -0.073 |
| Unemployment | -0.106 |

All four variables showed weak linear relationships with weekly sales.

Unemployment had the strongest relationship among the external factors examined, but the correlation remained weak.

---

## Business Recommendations

- Increase inventory and staffing preparation during **November and December**, when sales historically reach their strongest levels.
- Use high-performing stores such as **Stores 20 and 4** as internal benchmarks for further operational analysis.
- Investigate highly variable stores such as **Store 35** to determine whether fluctuations are related to seasonality, promotions, local demand, or operational conditions.
- Include **holiday indicators and seasonal features** in future sales forecasting models.
- Avoid relying on individual economic indicators such as CPI, unemployment, temperature, or fuel price alone for forecasting.
- Combine store characteristics, seasonality, holiday information, and economic variables in future predictive models.
